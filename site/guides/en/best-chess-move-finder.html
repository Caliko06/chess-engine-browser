Open calichess.cc, play the moves on the board, and it tells you what to play next, how good that move is, and why. No download, no account.

In one breathA chess engine is a program that imagines millions of ways the game could go, scores each one, and picks the move that leads to the best future it can prove. This one runs inside your browser tab.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Pick your side. White or Black, top right. The engine always answers for that side.
  2. Play the moves on the board. Click a piece, then the square. You move both sides, so you can rebuild any game.
  3. Read the panel. After each move, the right side shows what to play now. It takes about a fifth of a second.

What each part of the panel means

PartWhat it tells you
What to play nowOne sentence with the reason, for example "Play h5→f7: delivers checkmate."
Best ideaThe move it recommends, with its score in centipawns (hundredths of a pawn). Plus is good for you, minus is bad.
Other movesThree alternatives, numbered 2 to 4, also drawn as arrows on the board.
LineHow it expects the game to continue if both sides play well.
Depth and nodesHow far ahead it looked (depth 8 means 8 moves) and how many positions it checked.

It explains the move, not only the score

Most engines give you a number and a move. This one also writes a sentence: what your opponent threatens, what your move captures or attacks, and whether it is check or mate.

Aggressive mode

With Aggressive on, the engine still checks every option, but when two moves are almost equal (within half a pawn), it prefers the one that attacks the enemy king. You never give up more than half a pawn for a sharper game.

Why it runs in your browser

The engine is written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly, a format browsers run almost as fast as a normal program. The whole engine is a 191 KB download. In the browser it checks 1 to 1.5 million positions per second; that is why it has no server, needs no account, and keeps working without internet.
